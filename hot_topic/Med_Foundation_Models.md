- **[BigCell: Generating Gigapixel Whole-Slide Images](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14261)**  `arXiv:2610.14261`  `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Srikar Yellapragada, Alexandros Graikos, Zilinghan Li, Kostas Triaridis, Tarak Nath Nandi, Ji Dong K Bai, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Whole-slide images (WSIs) are gigapixel-scale digital scans of histopathology tissue and serve as the primary basis for cancer diagnosis. Existing histopathology generative models operate at the patch scale, producing fixed-size tiles of at most 1024$\times$1024 pixels which encompass only a small fraction of the WSI. In contrast, text reports for clinical diagnoses and slide-level diagnostic labels -- the supervision that exists at scale -- operate at the whole-slide level. We present BigCell, the first approach to generate coherent whole-slide histopathology images at native gigapixel resolution. BigCell trains a flow-matching transformer at 2048$\times$2048 pixels on over 120,000 publicly available WSIs spanning multiple organ types. Conditioning on embeddings from pathology foundation models and using an inference-time sliding-window pipeline, BigCell generates coherent images of up to 32,768$\times$32,768 pixels, outperforming prior tile generators on both tile-level and slide-level fidelity metrics. To make gigapixel synthesis practical, we introduce an adaptive scheduling strategy that prioritizes important slide regions, speeding up image generation by over 3\ttimes with minimal quality degradation. Furthermore, we propose the first text-to-image synthesis at 8k resolution by training a separate model that produces dense conditioning grids directly from pathology text reports. We demonstrate that augmenting with synthetic samples improves slide-level few-shot classification performance by up to $18 \%$ over baselines trained on real data.
  </details>

- **[LiFUSE: Frequency-Spatial Unified Self-Supervised Pretraining for Liver Disease Analysis on Non-contrast CT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18243)**  `arXiv:2610.18243`  `cs.CV` `cs.AI` `cs.LG`  
  _Hao Zhang, Yi Huang, Wei Liu, Yuan Gao, Zeju Li, Xu Han, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Given the growing global burden of liver tumors and diffuse liver diseases, non-contrast computed tomography (NCCT) is increasingly used for opportunistic screening because of its low cost and favorable safety profile. However, downstream tasks such as tumor detection and fine-grained disease diagnosis remain challenging due to NCCT's inherently low tissue contrast, lack of hemodynamic enhancement, and complex anatomical background. These limitations make subtle, heterogeneous, and multifocal hepatic lesions difficult to identify. Although large-scale self-supervised pretraining can learn robust representations from unlabeled NCCT scans, conventional voxel-wise reconstruction does not fully exploit the complementary information available in NCCT. Under weak NCCT contrast, low-level intensity patterns become less informative for pathology recognition, limiting the capture of lesion-specific discriminative features. In this paper, we propose the Liver Frequency-Spatial Unified Self-Supervised Pretraining (LiFUSE). The frequency branch applies random band-stop masking and spectrum reconstruction to encourage the model to capture subtle differences between lesions and surrounding tissues, while the spatial branch performs hybrid-granularity masked volumetric reconstruction to accommodate lesions of diverse sizes and morphologies. Finally, a cross-domain consistency scheme couples the spatial and frequency branches, yielding more discriminative representations for hepatic structures and lesions. Extensive experiments on three clinically relevant tasks across internal cohorts and a public target-domain transfer setting show that LiFUSE consistently outperforms strong baselines. These results suggest that unified frequency-spatial learning can improve liver disease analysis on NCCT, highlighting its potential for large-scale screening.
  </details>

- **[Beyond Sparse Captions: Aligning Slide-Level Text and Patch-Level Vision in Pathology](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17233)**  `arXiv:2610.17233`  `cs.CV`  
  _Daniel Shao, Dongmin Bang, Luca L. Weishaupt, Sophia J. Wagner, Nic G. Reitsam, Ming Y. Lu, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Contrastive vision-language (VL) pretraining in computational pathology has generally relied on curated image-caption datasets sourced from social media, educational videos, and research publications. These corpora are sparse relative to the diversity of tissue morphology and difficult to scale further, as pathologists do not routinely produce descriptive captions in clinical practice. We propose to instead leverage slide-level pathology reports which can be generated during routine diagnostic workflows in a scalable manner. A key challenge in this weakly-supervised setting is that pathology reports describe macroscopic, diagnostic findings while patch-level visual features describe microscopic findings. We introduce Locked Image Multiple Instance PreTraining (LIMIT), which addresses this gap by freezing a pretrained vision encoder and fine-tuning only the text encoder: report embeddings serve as cross-attention queries over bags of patch features, producing report-conditioned slide representations optimized via InfoNCE. Using LIMIT, we establish CONCH-Z, which aligns the text encoder of CONCH v1.5 to 335k clinical reports. Evaluated across 22 tasks spanning patch-level classification, tumor detection, and slide-level subtyping, CONCH-Z establishes state-of-the-art zero-shot performance over VL encoders trained on curated caption datasets, while simultaneously closing the gap with slide-level foundation models trained with substantially more parameters and compute. The code and model is available atthis https URL.

- **[FOCUS: A Subspecialty Foundation Model for Intraoperative Central Nervous System Pathology](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16914)**  `arXiv:2610.16914`  `cs.CV`  
  _Xing Liu, Yifan Guo, Hongtai Lv, Lei Lou, Yuyi Tan, Jiang Du, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Intraoperative frozen-section diagnosis of central nervous system (CNS) lesions guides surgical decisions but is challenged by artefacts, diagnostic complexity and time constraints. Most pathology foundation models have limited exposure to CNS frozen tissue. Here we present FOCUS, a subspecialty foundation model pretrained on 27,930 frozen-sections enriched for CNS tissue. FOCUS led 11 internal diagnostic and 12 biomarker-prediction tasks, with mean AUCs of 0.969 and 0.877, respectively, retaining leading performance in temporal and external cohorts. To provide more comprehensive diagnostic information, we further organized ten diagnostic tasks into a WHO-aligned diagnostic hierarchy and separately evaluated FOCUS for lymphoma detection, a critical task for intraoperative surgical decision-making. In reader assessments by six neuropathologists, FOCUS assistance enabled junior readers to approach or exceed unassisted senior readers in hierarchical diagnosis and raised junior readers' lymphoma sensitivity from 81.6% to 94.7%. FOCUS could improve diagnostic accuracy and granularity, inform lymphoma-related resection decisions and provide provisional molecular information. Such capabilities highlight the potential of FOCUS to provide robust, clinically actionable support for intraoperative CNS pathology.
  </details>

- **[Text-Guided Medical Image Segmentation via Residual Aggregation and Dense Alignment Representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14944)**  `arXiv:2610.14944`  `cs.CV`  
  _Jie Gui, Hang Tu, Yunfeng Dou, Wen Sha, Xiuquan Du, Shuo Li_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Specifying segmentation targets with natural language provides flexible semantic conditioning for medical image segmentation. However, existing methods still struggle to reliably ground high-level textual semantics into accurate pixel-level spatial prompts, especially under low-contrast lesions, ambiguous boundaries, and visually similar anatomical contexts. In this paper, we propose RADAR (Residual Aggregation and Dense Alignment Representations), a parameter-efficient framework for text-guided medical image segmentation. RADAR explicitly decouples and synergizes the medical semantic anchoring capability of BiomedCLIP with the prompt-driven mask decoding ability of MedSAM2, while enabling low-cost task adaptation via LoRA. Specifically, Guided Residual Depth Aggregation adaptively aggregates cross-layer features under semantic guidance to alleviate delayed textual injection and deep semantic dilution. Structured Dense Representation Alignment leverages a frozen BiomedCLIP to construct crop-level target, boundary, and context anchors, improving the local discriminability of dense features and the reliability of automatic prompts. Multi-modal Prompt Generation further converts cross-modal semantic responses into composite text, point, and mask prompts for MedSAM2 decoding. Experiments on three public medical image segmentation datasets show that RADAR achieves competitive performance with only 0.74M trainable parameters, demonstrating its effectiveness in parameter-efficient adaptation, semantic grounding, and dense representation enhancement. The source code is available atthis https URL.

- **[Streamline pathology foundation model by cross-magnification distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23097)**  `arXiv:2509.23097`  `cs.CV`  
  _Ziyu Su, Abdul Rehman Akbar, Usama Sajjad, Anil V. Parwani, Muhammad Khalid Khan Niazi_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Foundation models (FM) have transformed computational pathology but remain computationally prohibitive for clinical deployment due to their massive parameter counts and high-magnification processing requirements. Here, we introduce XMAG, a lightweight FM developed through corss-magnification distillation that transfers knowledge from state-of-the-art 20x magnification teacher to an efficient 5x magnification student architecture. XMAG employs a compact backbone and operates entirely at 5x, requiring 11.3 times fewer patches per whole slide image (WSI) compared to existing approaches. Our Novel distillation framework incorporates dual-level knowledge transfer, aligning both global image representations and local spatial token mapping. We trained XMAG on 3.49 million images curated from publicly available datasets and evaluated performance across six clinically relevant histopathology analysis tasks spanning multiple cancer types. XMAG achieved diagnostic accuracy within 1% of substantially larger foundation models while delivering 30-fold processing acceleration, reaching 8.8 WSIs per minute processing speed. Our cross-institutional validation confirmed robust generalization. Further, we developed an end-to-end training strategy to further boost our model's performance to approach the larger FMs' performance. These results establish cross-magnification distillation as a viable approach for deploying FM capabilities in resource-constrained clinical environments, potentially enabling real-time pathology AI integration.
  </details>
