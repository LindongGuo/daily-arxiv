- **[SemaNorm: Stain Normalization via Semantic and Morphology Alignment for Robust Cross-Hospital Computational Pathology](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00056)**  `arXiv:2611.00056`  `cs.CV` `cs.AI`  
  _Subhankar Chattoraj, Sawon Pratiher, Arijit Ghosh, Prashant Khare, Writankar Kundu, Karan Vishwakarma, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Stain variability remains a major source of domain shift in computational pathology, yet existing normalization methods largely optimize color consistency without explicitly preserving morphology or diagnostic semantics. We propose SemaNorm, a self-supervised stain canonicalization stain normalizer to recover clean patches from strongly perturbed inputs, avoiding paired transfer, adversarial instability, and identity collapse. It further combines a frozen pathology foundation-model consistency loss with a differentiable nuclei-structure loss to preserve semantic and cellular organization. SemaNorm is evaluated across Camelyon17-WILDS and NCT-CRC-HE-100K using cross-hospital tumor classification, cell-graph morphology metrics, and semantic stability measured with an independent held-out foundation encoder. SemaNorm improves out-of-distribution accuracy by 5.9% over the strongest normalization baseline and by 13.5% over no normalization, while achieving the best morphology preservation. Independent foundation-model and cell-graph analyses further confirm improved semantic and structural stability. These results establish morphology- and semantics-aware optimization as a principled alternative to color-only stain normalization.
  </details>

- **[CellPlex: Benchmarking Single-Cell Spatial Proteomics Prediction from H&E](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02506)**  `arXiv:2611.02506`  `cs.CV`  
  _Zijun Gao, Yijiang Chen, Xiangde Luo, Pheng-Ann Heng_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Spatial proteomics measures tens of proteins in every cell of a tissue section, but its cost confines it to small research cohorts, whereas hematoxylin and eosin (H\&E) slides exist for nearly every patient. Inferring single-cell spatial proteomics from H\&E would bring its analyses to routine pathology, and a growing number of virtual staining models pursue this goal. Each is evaluated on its own cohort, panel and pixel-level metric, and released models transfer poorly to new cohorts, leaving open which approach to build on and which proteins H\&E can reveal. We introduce CellPlex, a benchmark that poses the task at the level of the cell: seven cohorts with same-section H\&E and multiplexed immunofluorescence, from colorectal and breast cancer to lymph-node metastases, bone marrow and glioma, with 717 samples, 44.8 million cells and 149 proteins, processed by one pipeline into per-cell targets. Pathology foundation models (PFMs) are adapted with one cell-level protocol, specialist models are fine-tuned under a matched budget, and all are scored on the same cells. Adapted PFMs are the most accurate models on every cohort, ahead of the specialists even after fine-tuning, but keep less than half of their accuracy on cohorts they were not trained on. Which proteins can be predicted depends on the tissue, not on how many cells were measured for them. By fixing the cells, targets and splits on which predictions are judged, CellPlex sets a common standard for inferring costly molecular measurements from routine H\&E and shows how far H\&E can stand in for spatial proteomics. We will release the data, splits, evaluation code and baselines as a testbed for developing and comparing future pathology foundation models and virtual staining methods.
  </details>

- **[Vision-language classification of Torsades de Pointes risk from pseudo-coloured ECG images: do T-wave morphology and colour context help or hinder?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01921)**  `arXiv:2611.01921`  `cs.CV`  
  _Dario Labrador Alonso, Alaa Alahmadi_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Contextual prompting is widely presented as a cheap route to better performance from vision models, but the evidence comes largely from benchmarks in which an attribute named in a prompt is also present in the image, confounding the two channels. We investigate the significance of context for a semantically complex biomedical signal classification task using a domain-adapted vision-language model, BiomedCLIP. Building on our previous work that renders electrocardiogram (ECG) signals as colour-enhanced images to flag Torsades de Pointes risk using the QT-nomogram and pseudo-colouring technique, we cross four context strategies with four T-wave morphology label sets, evaluated zero-shot and with a class-balanced linear probe, under five-fold stratified cross-validation on 9,789 images. Zero-shot inference is unusable. Once trained, the colour-enhanced images outperform the colourless ones on every metric: balanced accuracy 0.903 versus 0.825, sensitivity 0.914 versus 0.827, specificity 0.892 versus 0.823, ROC-AUC 0.955 versus 0.888 and Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC) 0.437 versus 0.311. Crossing colour-in-image with colour-in-prompt separates the confounded channels: the image contributes +0.126 MCC (+0.078 balanced accuracy, +0.067 ROC-AUC) whether or not the prompt names colour, whereas naming it changes nothing under any metric. Context is two-sided: the best prompt set gains 0.008 MCC where colour is present and 0.016 where it is not, while the worst loses 0.017 and 0.013. For a signal rendered as an image, the leverage lies in how the signal is drawn rather than in how it is described - and where the endpoint is an interval measurement, prompts naming T-wave morphology describe the wrong quantity.
  </details>

- **[GESTALT: Fast, Generalizable Histopathology Image Synthesis from Gene Expression with Pretrained Pathology Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01141)**  `arXiv:2611.01141`  `cs.CV`  
  _Felix Ho, Alexandros Graikos, Dimitris Samaras_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Spatial transcriptomics measures gene expression while preserving where each cell sits in the tissue. The inverse, what a given transcriptomic state looks like, is less explored but equally useful: it lets researchers visualize the morphological consequence of a molecular state and interpret a spatial assay's molecular calls against real tissue structure. GeneFlow, the current state of the art on the Xenium gene expression-to-image benchmark, trains a model from scratch, incurring a high cost on every new target dataset it is applied to. We instead propose GESTALT, which reformulates gene expression-to-image synthesis as predicting a pathology foundation model's image embedding. A lightweight gene-token transformer predicts this embedding from multi-cell gene expression, and a frozen or LoRA-adapted diffusion decoder (PixCell) renders it as an image. Because this visual knowledge is reused rather than relearned, GESTALT trains in a fraction of the time. In both multi-cell and single-cell settings, our method outperforms the SoTA on perceptual metrics across all three Xenium datasets. Beyond improved image quality, GESTALT natively synthesizes at 1024 x 1024, and is evaluated in three generalization settings: zero-shot transfer across gene panels and datasets, held-out patients and disease states, and transfer across studies and tissue sources. Coupled with a statistical model of spatial expression, GESTALT also provides, to our knowledge, the first paired synthesis of cell-resolved transcriptomic fields and tissue images with explicit control over cell type, density, and composition. Code is available atthis https URL.
  </details>

- **[OpenMidnight: Closing the Performance Gap Between Open and Proprietary Pathology Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00064)**  `arXiv:2611.00064`  `cs.CV`  
  _Ratna Sagari Grandhi, Anis Chihoub, Ryan Kim, Daniel Z. Kaplan, Chao Feng, Anish Dulal, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Pathology foundation models offer transferable representations for a range of downstream tasks in computational pathology, but reliance on proprietary data, substantial compute requirements, and limited availability of training code and preprocessing pipelines hinder independent validation and further methodological development. We present OpenMidnight 1.1, an open-source replication and refinement of Midnight trained on approximately 12,000 publicly available whole-slide images from The Cancer Genome Atlas. Using an encoder initialized with pretrained DINOv2 weights, OpenMidnight 1.1 uses approximately 320 H100 GPU-hours for pathology pretraining, a 34-fold reduction relative to the compute reported for Midnight. Across 16 classification and four segmentation datasets in THUNDER, OpenMidnight 1.1 achieves the highest mean segmentation Dice (69.7) and ranks fourth among 14 models, ahead of Midnight, with performance that matches or exceeds models trained on substantially larger datasets in several tasks. Our ablation studies additionally examine how initialization, data volume, model size, and tissue composition affect downstream performance, providing empirical evidence to inform the development of future training recipes. Our release of the training code, preprocessing pipeline, and model weights lowers barriers to reproducible research and further development of competitive pathology foundation models.
  </details>
