- **[Multi-institutional grading of Barrett's esophagus dysplasia without stain normalization using a pathology foundation model and multiple instance learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26562)**  `arXiv:2611.26562`  `cs.CV` `cs.AI`  
  _Amirali Khosravi, Jaidip M. Jagtap, D. Chamil Codipilly, Catherine E. Hagen, Jason T. Lewis, Rickey E. Carter, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Barrett's esophagus dysplasia grading suffers substantial inter-observer variability, particularly for low-grade dysplasia, complicating clinical decision-making. We conducted a retrospective multi-institutional diagnostic accuracy study of a foundation model-based deep learning system for dysplasia grading, using 969 H\&E-stained whole slide images from four institutions. Patch-level classifiers trained on expert annotations identified diagnostically relevant regions, followed by attribute-aware multiple instance learning for slide-level classification as non-dysplastic Barrett's esophagus (NDBE), low-grade dysplasia, or high-grade dysplasia. On a held-out test set (n = 130), macro-averaged AUC was 0.96 (95\% CI 0.93--0.98). For binary dysplasia detection, sensitivity was 98\% (80/82) and specificity 81\% (39/48), with a negative likelihood ratio of 0.030 and quadratic-weighted \kap{} of 0.83 against expert consensus. No high-grade dysplasia case was misclassified as NDBE. Performance was consistent across institutions without stain normalization, indicating that stain-robust foundation-model representations may remove the site-specific preprocessing that has constrained prior systems to institution-specific deployment.
  </details>

- **[C-LiTE: A Lightweight Foundation Model for Cellular feature learning in Histopathology](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26316)**  `arXiv:2611.26316`  `cs.CV` `cs.AI`  
  _Esha Sadia Nasir, Jiaqi Lv, Gozde N. Gunesli, Shan E Ahmed Raza_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Characterising individual cells is fundamental to computational pathology, as cellular morphology informs tasks such as tumour grading, mitotic assessment and immune profiling. However, most pathology foundation models are trained on tissue patches and capture cellular features only implicitly, while existing cell-level models are either restricted to specific cell populations or rely on computationally expensive architectures with hundreds of millions of parameters. Applying ViTs directly to small cellular crops also presents an architectural challenge: upsampling increases computational cost without adding morphological information and may introduce interpolation artefacts. To address these limitations, we introduce C-LiTE, a lightweight foundation model for cellular representation learning in H&E-stained histopathology, with only 22 million parameters. C-LiTE employs a native-resolution tokenisation strategy tailored to cellular inputs, processing 64 x 64 px cell-centred crops using a 4 x 4 patch embedding to produce a 16 x 16 token grid without image upsampling. To transfer visual knowledge from large-scale pre-training, we adapt the patch embedding weights of a pre-trained ViT-5-S using a pseudoinverse-based transformation, enabling weight initialisation at the finer patch resolution. We further assemble a pre-training dataset of 60 million cell-centred crops from TCGA, balanced across 19 cancer types and 16 tissue types. To our knowledge, this represents the largest public dataset for self-supervised pre-training on H&E images at the cellular level. We evaluate C-LiTE on seven public datasets spanning mitotic figure recognition, nucleus and cell-type classification, and immune cell phenotyping. Under frozen-feature evaluation, C-LiTE achieves a mean macro-F1 of 0.670, approaching CytoFormer (0.682) with approximately 30x fewer parameters.
  </details>

- **[What Matters for Cross-Modal Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20000)**  `arXiv:2611.20000`  `cs.CV` `cs.AI`  
  _Yipeng Sun, Jiajun Wang, Siyuan Mei, Yan Xia, Siming Bayer, Vincent Christlein, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  The Platonic Representation Hypothesis posits that models trained on different modalities converge toward a shared representation. Related work aligns frozen image and text encoders on natural images without paired data. We test zero-pair alignment on five datasets across four modalities: natural images, environmental audio, chest radiographs, and 3D computed tomography (CT). It succeeds only on COCO and fails on the other four. We analyze the failures and find that alignment must first locate the subspace where the modalities co-vary and then orient the mapping within it. Consistent with previous studies, medical imaging is the hardest case: its shared subspace carries the smallest fraction of the variance. When this fraction is small, alignment needs image-text pairs, and random-matrix theory predicts how many pairs are needed. These findings motivate LightAlign: a few pairs let a ridge regression between two frozen encoders locate and orient the mapping simultaneously, and a classifier fitted only on report text then predicts findings from the mapped images. On 3D CT, LightAlign surpasses models trained end to end on image-report pairs without finding-level labels: with only 200 pairs, LightAlign outperforms CT-CLIP on CT-RATE (0.754 vs. 0.733 AUROC), and with only 100 CT-RATE pairs, it does so on RAD-ChestCT, an out-of-distribution dataset from a different hospital. On CheXpert, it reaches 0.862 mean AUROC with only about 5% as many pairs as models trained on MIMIC-CXR.
  </details>

- **[Are Vision Foundation Models Foundational for Electron Microscopy Image Segmentation?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08505)**  `arXiv:2602.08505`  `cs.CV`  
  _Caterina Fuster-Barceló, Virginie Uhlmann_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Although vision foundation models (VFMs) are increasingly reused for biomedical image analysis, it remains unclear whether the latent representations they provide are general enough to support effective transfer and reuse across heterogeneous microscopy image datasets. Here, we study this question for the problem of mitochondria segmentation in electron microscopy (EM) images, using two popular public EM datasets (Lucchi++ and VNC) and three recent representative VFMs (DINOv2, DINOv3, and OpenCLIP). We evaluate two practical model adaptation regimes: a frozen-backbone setting in which only a lightweight segmentation head is trained on top of the VFM, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) via Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) in which the VFM is fine-tuned in a targeted manner to a specific dataset. Across all backbones, we observe that training on a single EM dataset yields good segmentation performance (quantified as foreground Intersection-over-Union), and that LoRA consistently improves in-domain performance. In contrast, training on multiple EM datasets leads to severe performance degradation for all models considered, with only marginal gains from PEFT. Exploration of the latent representation space through various techniques (PCA, Fréchet Dinov2 distance, and linear probes) reveals a pronounced and persistent domain mismatch between the two considered EM datasets in spite of their visual similarity, which is consistent with the observed failure of paired training. These results suggest that, while VFMs can deliver competitive results for EM segmentation within a single domain under lightweight adaptation, current PEFT strategies are insufficient to obtain a single robust model across heterogeneous EM datasets without additional domain-alignment mechanisms.
  </details>
